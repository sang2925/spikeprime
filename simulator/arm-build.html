Mission 7 arm: build guide

An arm on port C that swings out across the front of the robot and nudges the red handle of the Mission 7 mycelium arm. You can print this page (Ctrl or Cmd + P).

This is a plan, not a tested design. Nobody has built it or run it on the robot yet. The part names and sizes are guesses. Check the notes at the end before you build, and ask a grown-up before the robot moves.

Before you start: two things to measure on the real model

  1. How high is the red handle above the mat? (Our guess: 3 to 4 cm.) The finger must end about that high.
  2. Lift the real arm by hand. Which way must it move to be "completely extended"? Write it down.

Build steps

Drag a picture to turn it around. The orange glow shows the pieces you add in that step.

1The arm and the L piece

Pieces: 2a (arm), 4 (L-shaped liftarm), 2 pins

  1. Lay the 15-hole beam (2a) flat. This is the arm.
  2. Put the L-shaped liftarm (4) on top of the arm's end. One leg lies along the arm. The other leg points down.
  3. Push two pins through both pieces. Two pins stop it twisting. One pin alone swings loose.

2The finger and the red tip

Pieces: 2b (finger), 7 (red tip), 2 pins

  1. Put the second beam (2b) next to the L's down leg, so it hangs straight down.
  2. Pin it with two pins.
  3. Push the red pin (7) into the finger's bottom hole.
  4. How long? The red tip should be 3 to 4 cm above the mat when the arm is on the robot. Finger length is about arm height minus handle height. For example, with the arm 16 cm up and the handle 3.5 cm high, the finger is about 12.5 cm. Use a shorter beam if needed.

3The motor and shaft piece

Pieces: 1 (motor), 5 (shaft piece)

  1. Stand the motor flat with its shaft pointing up.
  2. Push the piece with the plus-shaped hole (5) onto the shaft. It should sit snug.

4Join the arm to the motor

Pieces: the arm from step 2, 2 pins

  1. Lay the arm's first hole (the end with no L piece) on the shaft piece.
  2. Push two pins through the arm and the shaft piece.
  3. Turn the shaft by hand. The arm must swing level, like a door, without rubbing the motor.

5The shelf

Pieces: 3 (two 7-hole beams), pins

  1. Put the two shelf beams (3) side by side under the motor.
  2. Pin the motor's side holes to the beams so the motor can't slide or turn.

6The brace

Pieces: 8 (5-hole beam), pins

  1. Pin the brace (8) under the front of the shelf, slanting back and down.
  2. It will join the robot's frame in the next step. It stops the shelf sagging.

7Put it on the robot

  1. Find the top-front black frame, on the right side of the robot.
  2. Pin the shelf to that frame. The shelf must reach about 6 cm past the front of the frame. The motor shaft should end up about 12.5 cm ahead of the wheel axle and 6 cm to the right of the robot's middle.
  3. Pin the bottom end of the brace to the front face of the frame.
  4. Turn the shaft by hand until the arm points left, across the front of the robot. The finger hangs just left of the pusher, inside the robot's width. This is the resting position. Start every run from here.
  5. Plug the motor into port C.

The robot in the picture is a plain block. Your real frame has its own holes, so you may need to adjust where the pins go.

Test it (ask a grown-up before the robot moves)

  1. Motor only. Download gyro-mission6-7-11_routefix_creep_push28_arm.llsp3 to slot 19 (not slot 0). With the robot lifted off the mat, run it and watch the arm.
    • It should swing about 105 degrees, from pointing left to pointing forward and a little right, and then swing back.
    • Swings the wrong way? In the two motor blocks, swap counterclockwise and clockwise.
    • Doesn't reach far enough? Try 120 in both blocks.
  2. Handle only. Put the robot where it ends after the leafcutter push. Run the arm. Does the finger touch the red handle? Does it move the arm the way your hand test showed?
  3. Whole mission, 5 times. Write down for each run: did the ant reach the nest, did the leaves stay in, did the mycelium extend, did the window drop, did the robot get home.

What we still don't know

  • Whether the arm reaches the handle every time. In the simulator the handle ended up about 7 cm to one side or the other from run to run, and the arm covers only part of that.
  • The real handle height and the right finger length.
  • Whether the real arm needs to be pushed to the right. Your hand test answers that.
  • Whether port C has a motor free on the real robot.
  • Whether the tall shelf is allowed by the equipment rules and robot height rules. We haven't checked.
